As I write these lines, I feel a mix of emotions. At 22, prompted by a simple comment from a friend, I founded my first company. Four years later, although it wasn't a success, the experience taught me more than I ever imagined, introduced me to incredible people, and let me wear countless hats. Most importantly: I had fun.

Today, at 35, we have just successfully sold a venture. This time, the company was born with a more realistic vision: we didn't aim to change the world, just to offer a useful product. Throughout this journey, I deepened my passion for product development, planning, and team leadership. I also learned very clearly what I do not want to do.

This post isn't about the transaction itself, but about the lessons learned and the emotions experienced. It's not about the team, my partners, or the acquirer. It is a personal reminder of what I want to tell myself the next time I embark on a new venture—because I am sure there will be a next time.

---

### Enjoy the process and stay true to yourself

Know your strengths, embrace every opportunity to learn, and strive to be the best version of yourself.

> “Live as if you were living already for the second time and as if you had acted the first time as wrongly as you are about to act now.”  
> — *Viktor Frankl*

### Be authentic and seek balance

Surrounding yourself with people who highlight your strengths and point out your areas for improvement can transform your journey.

Remember to balance all dimensions of your life: nurture personal relationships and keep up with your hobbies.

### Looking ahead

It is time to assist in the transition, but with eyes set firmly on the future—knowing that I want to rediscover the spark of my 22-year-old self, yet with the wisdom gained along the way.

And above all, don't take life too seriously.
